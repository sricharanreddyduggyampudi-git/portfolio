Public Transport Ergonomics

(01) Brief
Role: Ergonomics Researcher
Team Project (Group Study)
Field: TSRTC Pallevelugu Fleet
Zaheerabad Bus Depot
Method: REBA Posture Scoring
Anthropometry & Spatial Mapping
Timeline: Research Paper 2024
Decades-old state transit cabins and saloons force drivers and passengers into severe biomechanical strain.
State transport buses serve as the daily circulatory system for millions of commuters and long-haul drivers across Telangana. However, outdated interior layout geometries expose drivers to chronic musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs) and passengers to hazardous ingress obstacles. Conducted as a collaborative field research initiative at the Zaheerabad Bus Depot, this study investigates TSRTC Pallevelugu buses—cross-referencing full physical bus dimensions against 16 anthropometric parameters, tracking crew physiological shifts, and calculating joint posture strain using Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA) scoring.
(02) Overview
EMPIRICAL, BIOMECHANICAL, ACTIONABLE. A field study identifying critical posture distortion zones in state buses (REBA scores > 11.0), proposing anthropometric architectural fixes for driver steering reach, mirror sightlines, and passenger boarding steps.
Assessment Framework: Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA Biomechanical Risk Scoring)
Field Study Focus: TSRTC Pallevelugu Fleet (Zaheerabad Depot & Rural Transit Routes)
Core Output: Dimensional Mapping, Anthropometric Correlation & Cockpit Redesign
(03) Posture & Task Analysis

Biomechanical Strain & REBA High-Risk Hotspots

Using multi-angle video tracking and observational posture grids, discrete operator tasks were evaluated using the Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA) framework. Any score above 11 indicates acute musculoskeletal risk requiring immediate redesign:

REBA: 11.75 · High Biomechanical Risk Driver Cabin Ingress Obstruction Restricted door width (58cm) and steep step height force severe torso torsion. Drivers actively avoid the dedicated driver door, choosing instead to squeeze through the passenger saloon and engine hump.
REBA: 11.25 · High Biomechanical Risk Side Mirror Sightline & Neck Twist Excessive horizontal offset (>95cm) between the driver seat datum and the exterior left mirror forces continuous neck hyperextension and lateral trunk bend to monitor boarding passengers.
REBA: 11.00 · High Biomechanical Risk Steering Diameter & Shoulder Extension Large 45cm diameter steering wheel combined with a flat mounting angle forces drivers into sustained maximum forward reach (>65cm), inducing static muscular fatigue across 16-hour shifts.
REBA: 11.75 / 7.9 · High Ingress Risk Passenger Entry Steps & Saloon Ingress Initial step riser height (38.5cm) vastly exceeds standard 5th percentile popliteal clearance. High physical strain during boarding (11.75) drops during disembarking (7.9), creating severe fall hazards for seniors and pregnant women.
(04) Dimensional Analysis

Measured Bus Geometry vs. Standard Indian Anthropometry

Physical dimensions of TSRTC Pallevelugu buses measured at Zaheerabad Depot cross-referenced against 16 standard anthropometric body dimensions (5th to 95th percentiles):

Bus Zone / Parameter Measured Geometry vs. Ergonomic Requirement & Mismatch Impact
Driver Ingress Door Measured 58 cm width / 142 cm height vs 76 cm / 175 cm requirement. Causes severe spinal contortion and head strike risk, leading to complete non-use by drivers.
Driver Steering Reach 45 cm wheel diameter @ 65 cm reach. Exceeds 5th percentile functional forward reach (56 cm), forcing constant forward lean off backrest support.
Passenger Step Riser 38.5 cm first step height vs 22–24 cm recommended maximum. Demands extreme hip and knee flexion during boarding, creating acute accessibility barriers.
Seat Pitch & Legroom 68 cm pitch / 25 cm knee clearance. Causes severe patellar impingement for passengers above the 50th percentile buttock-popliteal length (>48 cm).
Conductor Aisle Width 38.5 cm clear aisle width vs 55 cm biacromial shoulder breadth. Forces continuous sideways walking, shoulder collisions, and awkward torso twisting during ticketing.

(05) Crew Health & Physiology

Work Schedules, Musculoskeletal Disorders (MSDs) & Fatigue

Quantitative analysis of 100 surveyed transport crew members working 16–18 hour daily shifts across 15–20 consecutive days revealed alarming physical and physiological strain:

01 / Leg & Knee MSDs

93.3% Legs · 83.3% Knees

Prolonged static standing on moving buses exerts unilateral pressure primarily on the left leg, leading to severe joint pain, tingling (83.3%), and numbness (50.0%).

02 / Shoulder & Back Strain

80.0% Shoulders · 56.7% Back

Carrying heavy 2.0–2.5 kg ticketing cash bags on the left shoulder creates persistent asymmetric spinal compression and chronic trapezial fatigue.

03 / Cardiovascular Stress

BP: 119/80 → 136/97 mmHg

Post-trip physiological monitoring demonstrated statistically significant surges in systolic/diastolic blood pressure and heart rate (82 → 96 bpm, p < 0.05).

(06) Interventions

Ergonomic Spatial Interventions & Layout Optimizations

Based on the empirical findings, the research outlines actionable design interventions balancing immediate retrofit viability with long-term bus body manufacturing standards:

Driver Cockpit Redesign Angle dashboard controls within primary reach envelopes (45cm), reduce steering diameter to 38cm with power assistance, and position exterior side mirrors within a 30° horizontal vision cone.
Ingress & Step Architecture Replace steep 38.5cm risers with a 3-step progressive 22cm platform with tactile continuous yellow grab rails, lowering entry barrier for senior citizens and luggage carriers.
Saloon Clearance & Seating Increase passenger seat pitch from 68cm to 76cm with anatomical lumbar curvature, and widen central aisle from 38.5cm to 48cm to eliminate conductor-passenger impact zones.
