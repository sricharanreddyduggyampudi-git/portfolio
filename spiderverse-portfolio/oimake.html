OiMake STEAM

(01) Brief
Role: Product Design Intern
Company: OiMake (MakerVerse)
Hyderabad, India
Bridging the gap between passive screen consumption and hands-on physical making for children aged 8 to 14.

During my industrial product development internship at OiMake, I worked on the end-to-end design lifecycle for hands-on STEAM kits where children build, paint, and light up real functional objects. Designing for children requires solving a dual-stakeholder equation: the parent seeks educational value, screen-free focus, and safety, while the child demands immediate tactile excitement, independence, and pride in a finished usable creation.

(02) Overview & Impact
Tactile, solderless, and self-guided. Designing commercial physical STEAM kits from scratch, replacing passive screen time with hands-on cognitive making.
01

Memory Retention & Application

Children retain 75% of what they learn through practice and 90% when immediately applying knowledge through physical building (National Training Laboratories).

02

Cognitive & Spatial Development

Physical manipulation of 3D objects, layers, and circuitry strengthens neural pathways, spatial reasoning, and academic problem-solving confidence.

03

Fine Motor Skills & Dexterity

Precise tactile movements — component insertion, custom painting, slotting tabs, and threading — develop critical fine-motor coordination.

04

Critical Thinking & Problem Solving

Navigating physical assembly challenges fosters experimental hypothesis-testing, cause-and-effect understanding, and resilience without digital distraction.

05

Emotional Reward & Pride

Completing a functional, illuminated wearable or decor object provides a lasting sense of achievement, encouraging self-driven making habits.

06

Art + STEM Dual-Brain Integration

Combining visual arts with electronics simultaneously activates both analytical logic and creative expression in young learners.

Field Validation

Observational Children Workshops

Before defining product geometry, structured observation workshops were conducted with children aged 8 to 14 across school design clubs, domestic home environments, and after-school activity centers. The objective was to map the natural arc of a child's engagement with unfamiliar components, measure fine-motor dexterity limits, and identify instructional friction points.

✦

Tactile-First Instinct: Children touch, rotate, and attempt to assemble physical parts immediately before reading any written text. Products must reward this instinct with intuitive geometry.

✦

Social Dynamics & Comparison: When children build side by side, activity naturally becomes collaborative. Subtle variations in speed, light, and decoration trigger healthy peer discussion.

✦

Zero Adult Interventions: Instructional friction caused by small screws or complex text leads to parental takeover. The experience required 100% visual, step-by-step vector guidance.

Workshop Field Data:
Live workshop sessions in Hyderabad: Testing assembly sequencing, joint snap forces, and unassisted child engagement across 100+ young makers.
User Understanding

Primary Research: Kids & Indian Parents

Educational toy purchases in the Indian market are heavily parent-driven. A successful product must satisfy both the buyer (parent) and the user (child).

Parent Evaluation Factor Research Insight & Design Response
Screen Time Anxiety Parents actively seek screen-free alternatives. Providing a 45–60 minute focused physical activity addresses their #1 concern.
Tangible Learning Proof Parents look for visible outcomes: understanding simple circuits, mechanics, and creative customization that can be proudly displayed.
Accessible Price Point INR 500–1,200 represents the optimal sweet spot for trustworthy, impulse-friendly educational gifting.
Child Independence Busy parents value toys that children can complete without adult supervision or risk of electronic shock.
Core Design Process
Research → Understand → Explore → Evaluate → Develop → Prototype → Test → Iterate → Refine → Finalise

An iterative loop where prototype testing continually informs material selection, tolerance refinement, and graphic clarity.

01

Research & Market Understanding

  • Studied the existing Indian and international educational toy landscape to map market offerings.
  • Compared competing products based on features, price tiers, materials, electronics integration, and target age groups.
  • Identified a major market gap: no affordable (<₹1,000) Indian product combined wearable textile art with safe LED electronics.
  • Observed retail toy stores and activity centers to establish prevailing visual trends and child expectations.
Market Benchmark:
Retail toy store mapping, competitor landscape analysis, and price point gap identification in physical STEAM learning.
02

User, Ergonomic & Context Study

  • Studied target user physical abilities, hand reach, finger dexterity, and grip strength for ages 8–14.
  • Utilized standard anthropometric datasets (shoulder width 28–39 cm, back length 30–46 cm) to size the bag at 30×40 cm for comfortable universal wearability.
  • Evaluated how products are handled on tables during painting vs. worn when moving or mounted on metal surfaces.
  • Configured component dimensions so 5mm LEDs, slide pockets, and push switches can be handled effortlessly by young fingers.
Ergonomic Sizing:
Anthropometric child dimensional mapping and wearable bag ergonomics verified through unassisted child wearability trials.
03

Material & Technology Exploration

  • Tested multiple fabric substrates (muslin, bleached cotton, non-woven, felt) against paint absorption, bleeding, and durability.
  • Evaluated coloring mediums: acrylic fabric paints offered vivid colors; fabric markers provided maximum precision, zero mess, and non-toxic child safety.
  • Investigated lighting setups: compared DIP, SMD, and COB LEDs for luminosity diffusion, battery life, and durability.
  • Fabricated an adjustable testing jig to determine that a 6–8mm spacer depth between LED surface and diffuser produces optimal glow uniformity.
Materials & Modules:
Substrate paint absorption testing, fabric swatch evaluation, custom drop-in PCB modules, and battery pack integrations.
04

Concept & Visual Exploration

  • Explored diverse form factors (backpacks, slings, tote bags, circular magnets, motorized robots).
  • Developed child-centric illustration themes: space exploration, underwater marine life, off-road vehicles, dinosaurs, and botanical mandalas.
  • Engineered graphic compositions so critical illustration focal points align directly over backlight LED positions.
  • Maintained bold, clean line-work to ensure children can easily paint within borders without visual confusion.
Concept Ideation:
Laser-cut mandala patterning, optical backlight registration, and 3D kinetic mechanism concept explorations.
05

Evaluate & Select

  • Benchmarked concepts against core criteria: functionality, child assembly safety, tool-free construction, and BOM cost targets.
  • Eliminated direct-sewn circuits and loose wire bundles due to high failure rates and assembly frustration during testing.
  • Selected modular PCB + EVA foam pocket architecture for the backpack and layered laser-cut MDF for the fridge magnet.
  • Prioritized directions that delivered instant visual payoff upon completing the circuit.
Usability Evaluation:
Live unassisted child usability sessions evaluating component assembly, coloring engagement, and circuit completion.
06

Develop & Integrate

  • Designed precision laser-cut MDF nesting sheets that minimize kerf burn and optimize sheet utilization.
  • Engineered custom PCB layouts consolidating battery clips, switches, and LEDs onto a single rigid drop-in module.
  • Developed a 100:1 compound gear reduction transmission to convert high-speed DC motor rotation into precise 1-RPM timing mechanisms.
  • Integrated slit-disc optical sensors with LDR light-interruption logic for kinetic interactive toys.
Technical Engineering:
Optical diffusion standoff jig (6–8mm), modular PCB carrier, and 100:1 compound gear reduction drive mechanism.
07

Prototype & Test

  • Built functional physical prototypes across materials: stitched cotton bags, EVA pocket inserts, and multi-layer MDF stacks.
  • Conducted hands-on testing sessions with real children to observe assembly time, joint friction, and battery connection safety.
  • Identified critical real-world failure points: slide switches were confusing to slide, and rubber band wrapping caused layer misalignment.
  • Validated luminous diffusion through unpainted vs. painted fabric sections under varying ambient light conditions.
Physical Testing:
Assembly sequencing trials, mechanical friction testing, and luminous diffusion validation across light and dark settings.
08

Iterate & Refine

  • LED Array Upgrade: Replaced single center LED with a 5-LED distributed array to eliminate harsh hot-spots.
  • Switch Ergonomics: Substituted slide switch with a tactile push-to-toggle button to prevent accidental switching.
  • Assembly Sequencing: Introduced M3 screw alignment posts through MDF Layer 1 to serve as foolproof guide rails during stacking.
  • Instruction Redesign: Converted photographic manuals into 100% language-free vector step illustrations printed directly on packaging.
Refinement Loop:
Classroom validation of language-free vector assembly instructions, switch tactile feedback, and modular alignment posts.
09

Final Outcome & Commercial Launch

  • Consolidated research, DFM engineering, and iterative testing into four market-ready commercial STEAM kits.
  • Achieved rigorous retail Bill of Materials (BOM) cost targets while ensuring 100% unassisted assembly success in children aged 8+.
  • Successfully launched across Amazon India and integrated into maker workshop curriculums reaching 100+ young creators.
  • Packaged with full visual guides, premium laser-cut components, safe low-voltage electronics, and non-toxic craft supplies.
